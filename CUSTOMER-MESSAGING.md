# Customer messaging guide

The site leads with the visitor's occasion and desired outcome, then helps them choose a style, check fit and plan around the event date. Chess gives the brand character, but useful buying information comes first.

## Message sequence

1. **What is the occasion?** Wedding, black tie, prom, school event, family celebration or business.
2. **Who is getting dressed?** Men and kids have preview collections; Queen is in development.
3. **Which look fits the dress code?** Show relevant styles and complete outfit options.
4. **Will it fit?** Offer measurement instructions and product-specific questions, without claiming a guaranteed size.
5. **Will it arrive and be ready in time?** Ask for the event date; confirm stock, delivery and alterations before an order.
6. **What happens next?** Let visitors send an enquiry that includes the look they selected.

## Copy rules for launch

- Use clear, customer-facing phrases such as “Find your look,” “Check measurements,” “Ask about fit and timing,” and “Plan around your date.”
- Keep chess language as a distinctive accent, not as the only explanation of a product or action.
- Show verified policies, genuine reviews and actual customer photos only after they exist. Do not write sample quotes as social proof.
- Replace the preview labels only after real prices, stock, checkout, shipping, returns and tailoring terms have been approved and implemented.
- Replace the email-draft enquiry with a real CRM-connected form when a privacy notice and contact workflow are ready.

This applies the core idea from Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*: start with a desire people already have, match the explanation to what they know, and make a concrete mechanism and evidence do the persuading in a crowded market. For Checkmatela, that means event readiness, fit confidence and clear next steps rather than unsupported claims of perfection or speed.
